The planet's edge

EVE always puts the planet in the middle of the window, so only its size is needed: Click the edge, then click once anywhere on the planet's edge. Mouse wheel over the picture fine-tunes it. Zoomed in EVE after locking on? Click the edge again: it keeps the lock and works out the new distance. With this system's sky recorded, the edge is found by itself.

The stars and nebula behind the planet never move (David's idea), so the sky shows which way the camera faces, on every planet in the system, under any filter. Once per system: lock on below (any way), zoomed all the way out, then turn the planet slowly all the way round, a bit from above and below too: the sky is recorded as you turn (Scan doesn't need to be on). After that: open any planet in this system and it locks on by itself; scans stay on it.

Or point out buildings yourself
Quickest: zoomed all the way out with the edge set, click one of your buildings in EVE so your network shows. Freeze, pick one of your buildings that's listed ×1 (your Launchpad, the rocket icon, is easy to spot), click it on the picture, Lock on. Rings on the wrong side? Flip (it remembers).
Not your colony? Anyone's buildings work: in EVE right-click the planet → Show other characters' networks. Freeze, pick a kind of building below, click one of those on the picture (a magnifier helps). Look-alikes (two storage facilities) don't matter: the app works out which is which. Three or more spread out (extractor heads are good) tells which way the planet faces; afterwards green rings sit on every building.
4

Scan

Once per planet: zoomed all the way out, EVE on No filter, pick Bare planet below, press Scan and turn the planet all the way round (top and bottom too): the app learns the planet's own colours, so they don't read as resource. Then for each resource: in EVE's Scan panel drag both ends of the colour band right out (and leave them), pick the same resource here, press Scan, turn the planet round once, Save to SeAT. With this system's sky recorded, every picture is placed on the sky, so nothing slides (and without it, on the bare map).
